# Citation Policy

## Citation minimum

Every important historical, demographic, political, legal, or current-affairs claim must be connected to a source that a reader can inspect. Use the closest practical citation to the claim rather than a general list at the end of a page.

## Preferred order of evidence

1. Primary documents and artifacts: inscriptions, archival records, original treaties, laws, resolutions, contemporaneous maps, census tables, official transcripts, and dated testimony.
2. Peer-reviewed scholarship and academic reference works that identify their evidence.
3. Reports by institutions with a clear mandate and methodology.
4. Reputable journalism for contemporary events, especially where it supplies on-the-ground reporting or identifies primary records.
5. Advocacy and political statements, cited as evidence of what the organization said, not automatically as independent proof of the underlying claim.

No category is infallible. Primary sources can be incomplete, propagandistic, forged, mistranslated, or preserved only through later copies. Institutional sources should be read with attention to their mandate and limitations.

## Required metadata

For books and articles, record author, title, publication, year, page or section, DOI or stable URL. For web pages, record author/institution, title, publication/update date when available, URL, and access date. For legal decisions, record court, case title, date, paragraph number, and operative finding. For archaeological inscriptions, record site, catalogue number, date range, language, edition, translation, and uncertainty.

## Quotations and translation

Quote only as much as needed, preserve the original wording, use quotation marks accurately, and provide page or paragraph references. Identify the translator and edition. Do not silently modernize, combine, or paraphrase text as though it were a direct quotation.

## Claim labels

- **Documented:** a source directly attests the narrowly worded claim.
- **Supported:** multiple independent sources or evidence types support the claim.
- **Disputed:** credible sources disagree about interpretation or details.
- **Unverified:** evidence is insufficient or provenance is unclear.
- **Corrected:** the archive has changed a prior claim; preserve the reason in the correction log.

A claim may be documented in one narrow sense and still disputed in its broader interpretation. State the scope.

## Current information

Current events must include the date of the event and the date of publication or data update. Explain when a figure is preliminary, estimated, attributed, or revised. Do not present old data as current.

## Images and maps

Record creator, date, rights holder, license, source URL, and what the image depicts. Do not reuse copyrighted images or maps without permission or a license that allows the intended use.
