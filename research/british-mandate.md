# British Mandate Palestine, 1917–1947

**Status:** Introductory synthesis.  
**Scope:** British military occupation and administration, League of Nations Mandate, and the end of the Mandate.

## Key context

British forces entered Jerusalem in December 1917 during the First World War. The Balfour Declaration of 2 November 1917 expressed British government support for “the establishment in Palestine of a national home for the Jewish people,” while also stating that nothing should prejudice the civil and religious rights of existing non-Jewish communities or the rights and political status enjoyed by Jews in other countries. The declaration did not itself establish a state. Its meaning, drafting context, and relationship to other wartime commitments remain central to historical debate.

The League of Nations approved the Mandate for Palestine in 1922; the Mandate incorporated the Balfour Declaration and set out responsibilities for the administering authority. The period saw immigration, institution-building, economic and social change, increasing conflict, and competing national projects. Palestinian Arabs demanded political independence and opposed policies they understood as threatening their majority status and political future. Zionist institutions pursued a Jewish national home and, increasingly, statehood. The communities were internally diverse; neither should be described as politically uniform.

The 1936–1939 Arab Revolt was a major uprising against British rule and Zionist immigration and land-purchase policies, followed by severe British repression and lasting political consequences. The Second World War and the Holocaust transformed the international context and intensified the urgency of Jewish refuge and statehood. These facts must be examined together without using one community's historical suffering to erase another's rights.

In 1947 Britain referred the question to the United Nations. General Assembly Resolution 181 (II) recommended partition into an Arab state and a Jewish state, with Jerusalem under a special international regime. The recommendation was not implemented as a mutually accepted settlement; fighting escalated after the vote.

## Claims that require careful sourcing

- Immigration and population change: cite census years and explain the boundaries and categories used.
- Land purchases: distinguish registered private land, public or state land, cultivation, tenancy, and customary rights.
- Violence: attribute responsibility to named actors and incidents; do not flatten different events into a single undifferentiated narrative.
- Legal status: distinguish the Balfour Declaration, the Mandate text, UN recommendations, and later state declarations.
