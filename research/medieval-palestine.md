# Palestine in Late Antiquity and the Medieval Period

**Status:** Introductory framework.  
**Scope:** Byzantine period through Mamluk rule.

## Overview

Following the Roman period, the region passed through Byzantine rule and then came under early Islamic rule in the seventh century. Subsequent centuries included Umayyad, Abbasid, Fatimid, Crusader, Ayyubid, and Mamluk authorities, with changing borders, administrative arrangements, military campaigns, and religious institutions. The chronology was not a simple succession of uniform regimes: control varied across districts and cities, and local communities experienced different conditions.

Jerusalem held religious significance for Jews, Christians, and Muslims, while towns, villages, agricultural landscapes, trade routes, and holy sites connected the region to wider Mediterranean and West Asian networks. Arabic became increasingly central to public life over time, while Aramaic/Syriac, Greek, Hebrew, and other languages also appear in different communities and records. Religious conversion, migration, intermarriage, and language shift are complex processes and should not be reduced to a single event or population replacement.

The term Palestine continued to appear in geographical and administrative contexts, though boundaries and official nomenclature changed. A place-name's continued use is evidence of geographical and cultural memory; it is not, on its own, proof that a modern nation-state existed unchanged through every intervening period.

## Evidence strategy

Prioritize dated inscriptions, papyri, coins, tax and administrative records, travel accounts, chronicles, archaeology, religious endowments, and critical modern scholarship. Chronicles should be read with attention to authorship, audience, political patronage, and distance from the events described.
