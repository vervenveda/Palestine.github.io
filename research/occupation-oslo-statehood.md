# Occupation, Palestinian Governance, and Statehood, 1967–Present

**Status:** Introductory synthesis. Current facts must be updated with dated sources.  
**Scope:** 1967 war, occupation, Palestinian political institutions, Oslo, and international status.

## 1967 and its aftermath

In the June 1967 war, Israel captured the West Bank, including East Jerusalem, and the Gaza Strip, among other territories. The legal and political consequences of the occupation became a central issue in UN resolutions, diplomacy, and international litigation. A further wave of Palestinian displacement occurred. Descriptions of the occupied territory, settlements, annexation, movement restrictions, security, and civilian protection should be tied to dated evidence and applicable legal frameworks.

## Palestinian national institutions

The Palestine Liberation Organization (PLO) became a major representative body in Palestinian politics and diplomacy. In 1988, the Palestine National Council proclaimed the State of Palestine. In 1993, Israel and the PLO exchanged letters of mutual recognition and entered the Oslo process. The agreements created interim self-government arrangements and the Palestinian Authority in parts of the West Bank and Gaza, while deferring major permanent-status issues, including borders, Jerusalem, refugees, settlements, and security.

## Gaza and the West Bank

Israel removed its settlements and permanent ground forces from inside the Gaza Strip in 2005. Israel continued to exercise important forms of control, and Egypt controlled its side of the Gaza border; the legal assessment of Gaza's status after disengagement has been disputed, including in light of the degree of effective control. Hamas won the 2006 Palestinian legislative election and took control of Gaza after violent clashes in 2007, while the Palestinian Authority continued to administer limited self-government in parts of the West Bank. This political division has affected representation and governance.

## International status and law

In 2012, UN General Assembly Resolution 67/19 accorded Palestine non-member observer State status at the United Nations. This is distinct from full UN membership and does not, by itself, settle all questions of borders, recognition, or control.

In its advisory opinion of 19 July 2024, the International Court of Justice addressed the legal consequences arising from Israel's policies and practices in the Occupied Palestinian Territory, including East Jerusalem, and concluded that Israel's continued presence in the Occupied Palestinian Territory is unlawful. The opinion discusses Palestinian self-determination and the legal consequences of the occupation. It must be represented within its stated scope; it should not be paraphrased as a ruling that Israel has no statehood anywhere within its internationally recognized territory.

International humanitarian law and human-rights law are relevant to the conduct of all parties. Civilians retain protection; claims about specific violations require evidence, legal analysis, and precise attribution.
