# Palestinian Culture and Heritage

**Status:** Introductory framework.

## A living and layered heritage

Palestinian heritage includes built environments, archaeological sites, sacred places, rural and urban landscapes, traditional crafts, embroidery, music, foodways, oral literature, poetry, and contemporary artistic expression. Heritage is not only a record of ancient civilizations; it is also maintained and changed by living communities.

The region's heritage reflects many historical periods and religious traditions. A site may be significant to more than one community, and heritage claims should be grounded in reliable documentation rather than used to erase another community's relationship to it. Archaeological evidence can illuminate settlement and material culture, but objects alone do not always identify the language, ethnicity, or political identity of the people who used them.

## Documentation standard

For each site or object, record its name in relevant languages, location, date range, source of dating, archaeological or historical context, custodial authority, conservation status, and any disputed attribution. For images, record photographer, date, rights holder, license, and the exact subject. Avoid uploading copyrighted photographs or maps without permission or a clear license.

## Heritage and conflict

Conflict, displacement, development, neglect, and restrictions on access can threaten cultural heritage. Claims of damage or destruction should be supported by dated images, expert assessments, official reports, or multiple independent sources. Distinguish confirmed physical damage from reported damage, risk, and allegations under investigation.
